# Spectator (1st-order) vs Participant (2nd-order) Plane: Decomposing the Ratio Shift

**Observation.** Computing the coalescence ratio (the final observable, `ratio.pdf`) with
the 1st-order **spectator** plane gives a *significantly larger* $R$ than the default
2nd-order **participant** plane. This note decomposes *why*, isolating the two physical
channels that can move $R$.

The ratio is (`scripts/plot_v2_new.py`, see also [theory.md](theory.md)):

$$R = \frac{v_2^{\pi^-} - \tfrac{2}{3} v_2^{\bar p}}{v_2^{\pi^+} - \tfrac{2}{3} v_2^{\bar p}}
= \frac{N_{trans}^d}{N_{trans}^u}.$$

The spectator plane is more sensitive to electromagnetic-field effects (the $\mathbf{B}$
field is generated by the spectator protons and points $\perp$ to the reaction plane), so a
plane-dependent shift in $R$ is a probe of EM contamination of the coalescence signal.

**Scope.** This is a **side study**. The main observable of the analysis remains $R$ on the
2nd-order participant plane (`ratio.pdf`); nothing here changes that. The only question asked
here is: **which of two factors contributes more** to the spectator-plane increase in $R$ —
the **isospin splitting** $v_2^{\pi^-}-v_2^{\pi^+}$, or the **antiproton** $v_2^{\bar p}$. The
decomposition below reduces $R$ to exactly these two knobs ($\varepsilon$ and $\rho$), so the
deliverable is simply: per energy, how much of $\Delta R$ each one accounts for.

---

## Two candidate causes

The increase in $R$ can come from either:

1. **Enlarged isospin splitting** — the difference $v_2^{\pi^-} - v_2^{\pi^+}$ grows.
2. **Antiproton channel** — $v_2^{\bar p}$ shifts *relative* to the pion scale.

These are the only two possibilities, and the algebra below makes that statement exact.

---

## The ratio has exactly two scale-invariant degrees of freedom

$R$ is invariant under any **common** multiplicative rescaling of all three $v_2$ values —
which is precisely why the event-plane resolution cancels in $R$ (a shared factor on
$v_2^{\pi^+}, v_2^{\pi^-}, v_2^{\bar p}$). Dividing numerator and denominator by the pion
average $\langle v_2^\pi\rangle \equiv \tfrac{1}{2}(v_2^{\pi^+}+v_2^{\pi^-})$ collapses $R$
onto two dimensionless, resolution-independent quantities:

$$\boxed{\,R = 1 + \frac{\varepsilon}{\,1 - \tfrac{\varepsilon}{2} - \tfrac{2}{3}\rho\,}\,}
\qquad
\varepsilon \equiv \frac{v_2^{\pi^-}-v_2^{\pi^+}}{\langle v_2^\pi\rangle},
\qquad
\rho \equiv \frac{v_2^{\bar p}}{\langle v_2^\pi\rangle}.$$

- $\varepsilon$ = **fractional isospin splitting** = *cause 1*.
- $\rho$ = **antiproton-to-average-pion ratio** = *cause 2*.

A plane change can move $R$ **only** through $\varepsilon$ or $\rho$; nothing else survives.
This is the rigorous form of the two-cause picture above.

---

## Direction of each cause (signs matter)

**Cause 1 — isospin splitting.** Larger $\varepsilon$ raises $R$:

$$\frac{\partial R}{\partial \varepsilon}
= \frac{1 - \tfrac{2}{3}\rho}{\left(1-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}-\tfrac{2}{3}\rho\right)^2} > 0
\quad\text{(numerator } 1-\tfrac{2}{3}\rho>0 \text{ in the signal region).}$$

**Cause 2 — antiproton.** $R$ *increases with* $\rho$:

$$\frac{\partial R}{\partial \rho}
= \frac{\tfrac{2}{3}\,\varepsilon}{\left(1-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}-\tfrac{2}{3}\rho\right)^2} > 0.$$

> **Sign caution.** Because $\partial R/\partial\rho > 0$, a *relatively reduced* antiproton
> $v_2$ (smaller $\rho$) **lowers** $R$. For the antiproton channel to *raise* $R$, the
> antiproton must be relatively **enhanced** — suppressed *less* than the pions on the
> spectator plane — which shrinks the near-cancelling denominator
> $v_2^{\pi^+} - \tfrac{2}{3}v_2^{\bar p}$. A reduced antiproton and an increased $R$ are
> mutually inconsistent; if both are observed, the increase must be carried entirely by
> $\varepsilon$ (cause 1).

Numerical feel ($\varepsilon \approx 0.023$): at $\rho=1.26$, $R\approx1.16$; dropping
$\rho\to1.0$ gives $R\to1.07$; raising $\rho\to1.38$ gives $R\to1.33$.

---

## The diagnostic test, sharpened

The natural test — *"does $\langle v_2^\pi\rangle$ scale with the plane the same way as
$v_2^{\bar p}$?"* — is sound: equal scaling $\Rightarrow \rho$ unchanged $\Rightarrow$ cause 2
is off $\Rightarrow$ any $\Delta R$ is cause 1. Two refinements make it reliable:

1. **Propagate, don't eyeball.** The denominator $1-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}-\tfrac{2}{3}\rho$
   is a near-cancellation ($\rho\approx1.0$–$1.4 \Rightarrow$ denominator $\approx0.1$–$0.3$),
   so $R$ is *hypersensitive* to $\rho$: a $\sim3\%$ antiproton/pion scaling difference can
   move $R$ by tens of percent. Rather than judging "similar/not similar" by eye, compute
   $\varepsilon$ and $\rho$ on each plane and substitute into the boxed formula — this
   *attributes* $\Delta R$ directly.

2. **Use $\varepsilon$, not $R$, for the isospin double ratio.** The double ratio
   $R_{\text{spec}}/R_{\text{def}}$ mixes both causes. The clean, resolution-free
   EM-on-isospin observable is the **ratio of fractional splittings**

   $$\frac{\varepsilon_{\text{spec}}}{\varepsilon_{\text{def}}}
   = \frac{\big(v_2^{\pi^-}-v_2^{\pi^+}\big)_{\text{spec}} / \langle v_2^\pi\rangle_{\text{spec}}}
          {\big(v_2^{\pi^-}-v_2^{\pi^+}\big)_{\text{def}} / \langle v_2^\pi\rangle_{\text{def}}},$$

   which divides out the antiproton normalization.

---

## Procedure to attribute the observed increase

The ratio's input is `v2_eff_corrected.csv` (the $p_T/n_q \in [0.08, 0.6]$ GeV/$c$ low-window
v2, one row per centrality bin; the window integration happens upstream in the C++). The
default plane is the **2nd-order EPD** column; the spectator plane is the **1st-order EPD**
column of the `spectator_1st/` tree. Working **per centrality bin**, $\varepsilon$, $\rho$ and
$R$ are resolution-free (the per-bin resolution is a shared factor that cancels), so no
resolution-correction or centrality-merge is needed.

1. Compute $\varepsilon$ and $\rho$ on both planes, per bin.
2. Reconstruct $R$ from the boxed formula on each plane (consistency check).
3. Attribute $\Delta R$: hold $\rho$ at its default and vary only
   $\varepsilon\to\varepsilon_{\text{spec}}$ ($c_1$, cause-1 contribution), and vice-versa
   ($c_2$, cause-2 contribution).

**Exclusions** (per the analysis mask tables, `plot_v2_new.py`): drop **7.7 / 9.2 / 11.5 GeV**
(EPD merged ratios fully masked); drop statically-masked bins (17.3→bin1, 19.6→bins1,8,
27→bin1); and drop any bin whose denominator $D = 1-\varepsilon/2-\tfrac{2}{3}\rho$ is
consistent with zero ($D/\sigma_D < 2$) on either plane — there $R$ is Cauchy-like and
meaningless (cf. [ratio_statistics.md](ratio_statistics.md)).

---

## Result

Computed by `scripts/attribute_plane.py` (per-bin) and `--merged` (0–10 / 10–40 / 40–80%
classes, resolution-corrected). Tested against the null of *no plane effect* —
$\Delta\rho \equiv \rho_{\text{spec}}-\rho_{\text{def}}$ and
$\Delta\varepsilon \equiv \varepsilon_{\text{spec}}-\varepsilon_{\text{def}}$ vs zero, the
$(R{-}1)$ double ratio vs unity.

**At the level of individual bins or energies the effect is mostly not significant.** Per
energy the merged $\langle\Delta\rho\rangle$ reaches $\gtrsim3\sigma$ only at 14.6 and 19.6 GeV
(10–40% class); 17.3 and 27 GeV are individually consistent with no shift (1.3–1.4σ). The
$(R{-}1)$ double ratio is **consistent with unity** at merged level ($\chi^2/N \approx 1.1$) —
i.e. the $R$ increase itself is not robustly established once proper errors and the
final-measurement centrality merge are used.

What *does* survive, only by **pooling** all points:

| | per-bin (22 pts) | merged (11 pts) |
|---|---|---|
| $\langle\Delta\rho\rangle$ (antiproton) | $+0.035\pm0.007$ (5.4σ) | $+0.031\pm0.007$ (4.2σ) |
| $\langle\Delta\varepsilon\rangle$ (isospin) | $+0.002\pm0.001$ (1.3σ) | $+0.003\pm0.003$ (1.2σ) |

So **the isospin splitting $\varepsilon$ does not move; the antiproton $\rho$ shows a small
coherent positive shift** ($\sim\!+0.03$, i.e. $v_2^{\bar p}$ relatively *enhanced* on the
spectator plane — the sign consistent with $\partial R/\partial\rho>0$). The signal lives
almost entirely in the mid-central (10–40%) bins of 14.6 and 19.6 GeV.

**Conclusion (statistically honest):** at current statistics neither the $R$ shift nor the
antiproton shift is established outside two energies' mid-central bins. *To the extent anything
is resolved, it is the antiproton channel, not isospin* — but this rests on pooling and is not
a claim about any individual bin/energy.

**Caveats on the pooled significance.** (i) Errors treat the two planes as independent, but
they share events (only the plane angle differs) — the common fluctuation cancels in the
difference, which would *raise* the true significance. (ii) Within an energy the centrality
bins are not independent and carry common systematics, which would *lower* it. (iii) A naive
per-bin $|c_1|>|c_2|$ tally favors isospin (≈15:7) but is a red herring — inflated by
low-significance $\varepsilon$ swings where $\varepsilon_{\text{def}}\approx0$. A proper
correlated/paired error treatment (e.g. toy-MC resampling the shared events) is needed before
quoting a significance.
